# Enforcement, Search & Seizure, and Penalties under the BIS Act, 2016
**Issuing Authority**: Bureau of Indian Standards (Central Enforcement Wing)  
**Governing Statute**: The Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016 (Act No. 11 of 2016)

---

## Section 1: Prohibition of Improper Use of the Standard Mark
### Clause 1.1: Section 14 — Prohibition of Misuse
No person shall use the Standard Mark (ISI Mark, Hallmark, or CRS Registration Mark) or any colorable imitation thereof without a valid licence or certificate granted by the Bureau of Indian Standards.

### Clause 1.2: Section 15 — False Statements and Misleading Marks
No person shall make any representation with respect to the standard mark or conformity to an Indian Standard which is false or misleading in any material particular.

---

## Section 2: Powers of Search, Seizure and Inspection
### Clause 2.1: Search and Seizure under Section 17
BIS Inspecting Officers, accompanied by police personnel, possess statutory powers to:
- Enter and search any factory, godown, retail premises, or transport vehicle where there is reasonable suspicion that counterfeit or unauthorized goods bearing the BIS mark are produced, stored, or sold.
- Seize articles, packaging materials, dies, stamps, labels, and records relevant to the unauthorized manufacture or sale.
- Seal premises or testing equipment utilized in the commission of the offence.

---

## Section 3: Legal Penalties under Section 29
### Clause 3.1: Contravention of Section 14 or 15 (Misuse of Mark)
Any person who contravenes the provisions of Section 14 or 15 shall be punishable with:
1. Imprisonment for a term which may extend to **two years**, OR
2. Fine which shall not be less than **Rs. 2,00,000 (Rupees two lakhs)**, which may extend up to **ten times the value of goods** produced, sold, or offered for sale, OR
3. Both imprisonment and fine.

### Clause 3.2: Contravention of Mandatory Quality Control Orders (Section 16)
Any person who produces, imports, sells, or stores goods covered under mandatory certification without a valid licence shall be punishable with:
- First offence: Fine of not less than Rs. 5,00,000 for large enterprises and Rs. 1,00,000 for MSMEs.
- Second or subsequent offence: Imprisonment up to two years and fine up to ten times the value of goods.

---

## Section 4: Compounding of Offences (Section 33)
### Clause 4.1: Provisions for Compounding
Offences punishable under the BIS Act, 2016 (excluding offences committed by repeat offenders within a period of three years) may be compounded by the Competent Authority either before or after the institution of the prosecution:
- Compounding fee is payable in addition to forfeiture of seized non-conforming goods.
- Upon payment of compounding fees, criminal proceedings against the accused entity are dropped.

---

## Section 5: Jurisdiction of Courts
### Clause 5.1: Cognizance of Offences
No court inferior to that of a Metropolitan Magistrate or a Judicial Magistrate of the First Class shall try any offence punishable under the BIS Act.

### Clause 5.2: Prosecution by BIS
Courts take cognizance of an offence under the BIS Act only upon a formal complaint in writing made by the authorized officer of the Bureau or the Central/State Government.
