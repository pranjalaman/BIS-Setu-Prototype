# Indian Standards Formulation Process & Technical Committees
**Issuing Authority**: Bureau of Indian Standards (Standards Promotion & Technical Department)  
**Governing Statute**: Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016 (Chapter III — Standardization)

---

## Section 1: The National Standards Body Mandate
### Clause 1.1: Core Role
Under the BIS Act, 2016, BIS is designated as the National Standards Body of India responsible for the harmonious development of standardization, marking, and quality certification of goods, services, systems, and processes.

### Clause 1.2: Division Councils
Standards formulation is organized under 15 Division Councils covering distinct economic and industrial sectors, including:
1. Civil Engineering (CED)
2. Electrotechnical (ETD)
3. Electronics & Information Technology (LITD)
4. Chemical (CHD)
5. Food & Agriculture (FAD)
6. Mechanical Engineering (MED)
7. Petroleum, Coal & Related Products (PCD)
8. Medical Equipment & Hospital Planning (MHD)
9. Management & Systems (MSD)
10. Textiles (TXD)

---

## Section 2: Stages in the Formulation of an Indian Standard
### Clause 2.1: Stage-by-Stage Process
The development of an Indian Standard follows a transparent, consensus-driven six-stage cycle:
1. **Stage 1 (New Work Item Proposal - NWIP)**: Proposal submitted by industry, government department, consumer organization, or BIS officer.
2. **Stage 2 (Working Draft)**: Formulated by a specialized Sectional Committee or working group.
3. **Stage 3 (Committee Draft)**: Deliberated upon by sectional committee members comprising industry experts, technologists, and consumer representatives.
4. **Stage 4 (Draft for Wide Circulation)**: The approved draft is published on the BIS website for public consultation and comments for a minimum period of 60 days.
5. **Stage 5 (Finalization)**: Comments received during wide circulation are reviewed, and the draft is finalized by the Sectional Committee.
6. **Stage 6 (Adoption and Publication)**: Formally adopted by the Division Council Chairman and published in the Official Gazette of India as an Indian Standard.

---

## Section 3: Harmonization with International Standards
### Clause 3.1: Alignment with ISO and IEC
To support international trade and eliminate technical barriers to trade (TBT), Indian Standards are harmonized wherever practicable with standards issued by:
- International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
- International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC)
- International Telecommunication Union (ITU)

### Clause 3.2: Dual Numbering Convention
When an ISO or IEC standard is adopted identically or with national modifications, it is published with dual numbering (e.g., IS/ISO 9001:2015, IS/IEC 60601-1).

---

## Section 4: Periodic Review and Revision
### Clause 4.1: Five-Year Review Cycle
Every published Indian Standard is reviewed by the concerned Sectional Committee at least once every five years to determine whether it should be:
- Reaffirmed (continued without changes)
- Revised (updated to incorporate technological advancements)
- Withdrawn (if obsolete or replaced by newer specifications)
