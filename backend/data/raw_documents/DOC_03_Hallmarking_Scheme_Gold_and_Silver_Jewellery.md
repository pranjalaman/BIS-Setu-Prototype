# BIS Hallmarking Scheme for Gold and Silver Jewellery & Artefacts
**Issuing Authority**: Bureau of Indian Standards (Hallmarking Department)  
**Governing Regulation**: BIS (Hallmarking) Regulations, 2018 & Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution

---

## Section 1: Overview of Mandatory Hallmarking
### Clause 1.1: Objective of Hallmarking
Hallmarking is the accurate determination and official recording of the proportionate content of precious metal in gold and silver articles. It protects consumers against adulteration and obligates jewellers to maintain legal standards of fineness.

### Clause 1.2: Mandatory Status in India
The Government of India has made hallmarking of gold jewellery and artefacts mandatory in over 340+ districts across India. No jeweller in these designated districts is permitted to sell gold jewellery unless it bears the official BIS Hallmark.

---

## Section 2: The Three Marks on BIS Hallmarked Gold Jewellery
### Clause 2.1: The Three Mandatory Signs
Since 1st July 2021, all BIS hallmarked gold jewellery carries exactly three distinct laser marks:
1. **BIS Standard Mark**: The triangular BIS emblem / logo.
2. **Purity / Fineness Grade**: Numeric fineness in parts per thousand (e.g., 916 for 22K, 750 for 18K, 585 for 14K).
3. **HUID (Hallmark Unique Identification)**: A 6-digit alphanumeric unique laser code (e.g., AB12CD).

### Clause 2.2: Discontinuation of Jeweller's Logo & Year Code
Old hallmarking marks (which previously included the jeweller's identification mark and assaying center logo) have been replaced by the HUID system to ensure complete traceability and eliminate fraud.

---

## Section 3: Permissible Purity Grades for Gold Jewellery
### Clause 3.1: Officially Recognized Carats (IS 1417)
Under IS 1417 (Gold and Gold Alloys, Jewellery/Artefacts — Fineness and Marking), gold jewellery can only be hallmarked in the following standard grades:
- **24 Karat (999)**: 99.9% pure gold (typically for gold coins and bullion).
- **23 Karat (958)**: 95.8% pure gold.
- **22 Karat (916)**: 91.6% pure gold (standard for traditional Indian bridal jewellery).
- **20 Karat (833)**: 83.3% pure gold.
- **18 Karat (750)**: 75.0% pure gold (popular for diamond and studded jewellery).
- **14 Karat (585)**: 58.5% pure gold (modern daily wear jewellery).
- **9 Karat (375)**: 37.5% pure gold.

---

## Section 4: What is HUID (Hallmark Unique Identification)?
### Clause 4.1: Technical Nature of HUID
HUID is a 6-digit alphanumeric code assigned automatically by the central BIS Hallmarking server to every individual jewellery piece. It is laser etched at an Assaying and Hallmarking Centre (AHC) after testing.

### Clause 4.2: Security & Traceability Features
- Every piece of jewellery has a unique identity in the central national database.
- It prevents re-use of hallmarking certificates on duplicate or lighter jewellery pieces.
- Consumers can verify the legitimacy of any jewellery item instantly using the **BIS Care Mobile App**.

---

## Section 5: Jeweller Registration and Exemptions
### Clause 5.1: Automatic Online Registration
Any jeweller can obtain BIS registration online through the Manakonline portal without any physical inspection of the shop. No government registration fee is charged from small jewellers to facilitate ease of doing business.

### Clause 5.2: Specific Exemptions from Mandatory Hallmarking
The following are exempt from mandatory hallmarking:
1. Jewellers whose annual business turnover is up to Rs. 40 Lakhs.
2. Jewellery manufactured for export purposes (as per Foreign Trade Policy).
3. Gold items weighing less than 2 grams per piece.
4. Watches, pens, or special artefacts having negligible gold content.
5. Remelting and alteration of old unhallmarked gold jewellery brought by consumers (until it is resold).

---

## Section 6: Consumer Rights, Redressal, and Compensation
### Clause 6.1: Verification by Consumer
Consumers have the legal right to:
- Demand a magnifying glass or 10x loupe from the jeweller to inspect the 3 marks and HUID.
- Check the HUID on the **BIS Care App** under "Verify HUID", which displays the jeweller's name, AHC name, registration date, article type, and purity grade.

### Clause 6.2: Testing Rights of Consumers at AHC
A consumer can get any hallmarked gold article tested at any BIS-recognized Assaying and Hallmarking Centre (AHC) on priority by paying a nominal consumer testing fee (approx. Rs. 45 per article).

### Clause 6.3: Mandatory Compensation for Purity Deficit
If a hallmarked jewellery piece is tested and found to have a lower purity than marked:
- The consumer is entitled to compensation equal to **two times the shortage in purity** calculated on the weight of the article at current market rate.
- The selling jeweller is legally bound to pay this compensation and faces cancellation of BIS registration and prosecution under the BIS Act, 2016.

---

## Section 7: Silver Hallmarking Purity Standards (IS 2112)
### Clause 7.1: Recognized Silver Grades
Silver articles can be hallmarked under IS 2112 in 6 recognized grades:
- 990 (Fine Silver)
- 970 (Silver)
- 925 (Sterling Silver — standard for silverware and jewellery)
- 900 (Silver)
- 835 (Silver)
- 800 (Silver)
